Project Description

In 2021, Principal Investigator Dr. Shannon M. Monnat and the Lerner Center for Public
Health Promotion and Population Health at Syracuse University started to administer the
National Wellbeing Survey (NWS). This annual, web-based survey collects information
pertaining to the wellbeing of non-institutionalized adults aged 18-64 living in the United States.
Responses from 4014 respondents were collected.
Nastassia Vaitsiakhovich, Dr. Scott D. Lanes, and Dr. Monnat collaborated to publish The
role of perceived social support in subjective wellbeing among working-age U.S. adults with and
without limitations in activities of daily living. The objective of their study is to examine the role
of perceived emotional and instrumental support among three wellbeing domains among U.S.
adults living with and without activities of daily living (ADL) limitations. Emotional support
includes “empathy, consolation, and encouragement” (Weber and Patterson, 1996), whereas
instrumental support includes “providing tangible aid or goods” (Langford et al., 1997). The
three wellbeing domains comprise of hedonic, eudaimonic, and evaluative wellbeing. Happiness,
meaning in life, and life satisfaction describe these domains, respectively. The authors state that
the effects of social support on those with and without ADL limitations are not well understood.
While higher subjective wellbeing has been linked with better health outcomes, the literature has
largely focused on older age adults, not working age adults. Findings from studies from the
United Kingdom suggest that those with disabilities have lower subjective wellbeing compared
to their non-disabled counterparts. It is important to note that these differences may not stem
from the disabilities themselves but from a need for greater support. Insufficient support may
lead to a decrease in ability to exercise autonomy, which then may lead to negative effects on
subjective wellbeing.
Those living with ADL limitations are at a higher risk of developing mental health
symptoms related to illnesses such as depression (Yan et al., 2025). Living with an ADL
limitation or mental health challenge can have significant effects on the individual’s quality of
life (Wu et al., 2024). Current literature that explores the intersection of ADL limitations and
mental health challenges largely focuses their analyses on older adults and stops at the
establishment of their interplay. 
The results of this study may equip health care providers and living assistants with evidence-based
lifestyle interventions to enhance the wellbeing and quality of life of working-age adults living
with ADL limitations and mental health challenges.

Data Source

This project analyzes data from the 2021 National Wellbeing Survey (United States). Due to licensing restrictions and ICPSR Terms of Use, the raw dataset cannot be re-hosted in this repository.

To run this code, you must download the public-use dataset directly from the source:
Repository: Inter-university Consortium for Political and Social Research (ICPSR)
Study Link: [ICPSR Study #38879 (National Wellbeing Survey, 2021)](https://www.icpsr.umich.edu/sites/nahdap/view/studies/38879)

*Please Note: Version used for this project has been unpublished. It is no longer available for download. Ensure that column variable names match.

How to Setup the Data
1. Visit the ICPSR link above and log in (or create a free account).
2. Download the dataset in CSV format.
3. Extract the downloaded files and place the raw data file into the correct directory of this project.
4. Ensure the file is named `National Wellbeing Survey Public Use Data.csv`
